# FoldForm: an outside critique before the App Store submission

_Written 2026-11-01 for the submission of build 54 (v0.3.0) by an
independent reviewer at the owner's request. It covers the repository
(read-only), the listing texts and screenshots, the TestFlight state in the
docs, and the App Store and web as they were on 2026-11-01. Nothing was
built or run. Sales figures are estimates, labelled as such._

The owner's framing: "if I earn $10 I'll be shocked and if I earn $200 it
will be a huge success ... with the duo being so new that's my biggest
hope." This page takes both halves seriously.

---

## 1. What it is, and who pays $9.99

**What it is.** FoldForm is real solid-modeling CAD (the OpenCASCADE B-rep
kernel, an editable step history, constrained sketches, sizes typed with
units and arithmetic) built for touch, with helpers made for 3D printing:
screw-clearance and tap holes with a print allowance, heat-set insert holes,
raised text, and STL/3MF/STEP export. On iPhone and iPad it is free with
every tool for one project, a one-time USD 9.99 unlock allows more, and the
same program is free and unlimited on Windows.

**Who would actually pay.** A hobbyist with an FDM printer who designs
*functional* parts (brackets, enclosures, spacers, replacement clips) at
least a couple of times a month, wants to keep those parts editable on an
iPad or an iPhone Duo, will not pay a subscription for a hobby, and has
looked at Shapr3D's Pro price (USD 299 a year) and closed the tab. A
second, smaller group: Duo owners who just paid USD 1,999 and are looking
for apps that use the big screen.

**Who will not pay**, and this is most people who download it:

- People who print the odd part. The free tier gives them every tool and
  unlimited full-resolution exports; they make one part, export it, delete
  the project and start the next. Nothing in the app pushes back on that.
- People who mostly remix downloaded models: FoldForm cannot import an STL
  or 3MF (no mesh import anywhere in `src/`; STEP only).
- Engineers: they have Onshape, Fusion or Shapr3D on a computer.

So the buyer is "a person who builds a library of parts", and the price is
really a convenience fee plus goodwill. That is a legitimate model, but it
means conversions will come from people who like the app enough to keep
using it, not from a wall.

## 2. The product

### What is strong

- **The engine is genuinely good for a solo project.** About 68,800 lines of
  C++/QML/Objective-C++ in `src/`, about 31,000 lines of GoogleTest, and
  13,700 lines of real-UI acceptance scenarios (`src/app/acceptance/`);
  844 headless tests and 4,356 real-UI checks passing on 2026-10-31
  (PROJECT_STATUS.md, "Tests currently passing"). Kernel results are checked
  against the intent (shell must remove volume, offset must change volume by
  about area x distance), kernel faults become failed steps instead of
  crashes (`src/geometry/internal/KernelUtil.h:61-93`), project files are
  fuzzed, and failures say why in plain words, often with a size that works.
  Most indie CAD apps have none of this.
- **The sizing model is right for makers.** Tapping a face shows the
  part's size to the opposite face, not a delta (ROADMAP.md, Milestone 0,
  step 11); `+5` / `-5` are relative; `20+5`, `1in`, `2,5cm` all work.
  "Type what the calipers say" is exactly the job.
- **Print helpers that competitors do not have together:** ISO clearance
  and tap sizes M2 to M6 with counterbores and countersinks and a print
  allowance, heat-set insert presets, raised or engraved text, "lay a face on
  the build plate", and 3MF that keeps each body a separate object.
- **Trust signals makers care about:** no account, offline, "Data Not
  Collected" is true (APP_STORE.md, section 2), open source, a free Windows
  version, recovery copies after a crash.
- **The release machinery is careful:** a license gate on the iOS link map,
  the iOS sources attached to the v0.3.0 release, a written source offer in
  About, screenshots checked for size and alpha. The submission paperwork is
  better than many funded teams'.

### The real workflow: make a part, size it, export a printable STL

Judged from the review path (APP_STORE.md:688-695), the phone test list
(docs/IPAD.md, "What to test on the iPhone") and the code:

1. **Make.** Box gives a 20 mm cube; Sketch on the ground or a face, draw a
   rectangle and circles, Finish, tap inside, type a depth (negative cuts).
   This works and is fast once known.
2. **Size.** Tap a face, tap the value, FoldForm's numeric keypad appears,
   type, check mark. Good. Changing an earlier step goes through the Model
   panel, which on a phone slides in over the model, so you edit a value
   without seeing much of the part.
3. **Export.** File → Export STL writes `Exports/<name>.stl` and opens the
   share sheet (docs/IPAD.md:233-248). Mesh quality is fine for printing:
   0.01 mm chord, 0.2 rad angle (`src/geometry/Exchange.h:63-67`).
4. **Print.** This is where every iOS CAD app's story thins out: there is
   no mainstream full slicer on iPhone, so for most people "export" means
   AirDrop to the computer that runs the slicer. Shapr3D's new iPhone viewer
   advertises a Prusa EasyPrint integration for this last mile. FoldForm's
   share sheet is the honest minimum; it is worth testing which printer
   apps (Bambu Handy, Prusa, OctoPrint clients) accept a 3MF from it and
   saying so on the product page.

**Verdict on the workflow:** it is complete and correct. The problem is not
whether a part can be made; it is whether a first-time user finds out how.

### Where a phone-sized CAD UI breaks down here

- **Discovery.** The tools are text-only buttons (TD-9,
  docs/TECHNICAL_DEBT.md:16) in a sideways-scrolling strip. In the store's
  own first screenshot the strip shows six tools and then a clipped "S"
  (`docs/appstore/screenshots/iphone-1-project-box.png`); the other twenty-odd
  tools are off screen with no hint they exist.
- **Selection.** The owner himself, on his own Duo, reported "cannot
  figure out how to select a line" (PROJECT_STATUS.md:91-95). A fix shipped
  in build 54 but has not been seen on a device (LAUNCH_CHECKLIST.md:39-41).
  If the developer could not find it, a first-time user will not.
- **Gesture grammar.** Multi-select is "double-tap one, double-tap the
  other, or tap the other once" (docs/IPAD.md:598); undo is a two-finger tap,
  redo a three-finger tap. All learnable, none discoverable, and there is
  no onboarding, no sample project and no template anywhere in
  `src/ui/qml/` (Home offers New project, Open..., Import STEP...).
- **Screen budget.** With a face selected, the top bar, the value box and a
  row of actions (Shell, Offset, Align, Delete face, Select body) take the
  top third of a 6.9" screen; with the keypad up, the bottom half goes too.
- **Desktop vocabulary.** File menu, Save / Don't Save, "Open...", "Select
  body", "2 DOF" in the Model panel (ipad-1 screenshot), "Not fully sized" in
  the sketch. A maker on a phone expects autosave (TD-51,
  docs/TECHNICAL_DEBT.md:60) and plain words.
- **Smaller:** no dark mode (`src/ui/qml/Theme.qml` has one palette),
  Apple Pencil Scribble not expected to work (TD-90), no VoiceOver work (no
  `Accessible` properties in any QML file).

### What is missing for the stated buyer

| Missing | Who has it | Why it matters to a maker |
|---|---|---|
| STL / 3MF / OBJ import | OpenShape3D (STL, DXF), Jigsmith (STL, 3MF, OBJ, PLY, USDZ), Shapr3D (STL) | Remixing a downloaded part ("make this 5 mm taller, add a hole") is half of hobby 3D printing |
| Templates / sample parts | Jigsmith ships ten sample parts | The fastest way to show "every step stays editable" in ten seconds |
| Printer bed outline / fit check | Jigsmith | "Will it fit on my A1 mini" is asked of every part |
| Modeled threads, helix, sweep | OpenShape3D (helix, sweep) | Screw caps, threaded knobs |
| Section view | OpenShape3D | Checking a wall inside an enclosure |
| Projects on iPhone and iPad both | Shapr3D (sync) | The unlock covers "your iPhone and iPad", but projects live in each device's local folder (On My iPhone); moving them is manual |
| Autosave | Shapr3D, Jigsmith, almost every iOS app | iOS ends apps in the background; recovery copies help, but the model is still desktop-style |

None of these blocks a submission. Together they explain why a maker would
try FoldForm and then go back to what they had.

**Where USD 9.99 once fits:** it is the cheapest precise-CAD unlock in the
list: a third of Jigsmith's yearly, a sixth of its lifetime, a thirtieth of
Shapr3D's year. Price is not the obstacle; nobody will decline FoldForm for
costing ten dollars. Do not lower it. Proceeds are about USD 8.49 a sale at
the Small Business Program's 15% (about 6.99 at 30% until the enrollment
takes effect), so 12 sales cover the USD 99 membership.

**Realistic first-year sales (an estimate, with the reasoning shown).**
3D Print Designer CAD has 34 ratings after five months; if the usual very
rough ratio of one rating per 50 to 200 downloads holds, that is a few
thousand downloads. OpenShape3D (4 ratings in three months, free, no
marketing) suggests a few hundred. FoldForm starts with zero ratings, a
name nobody searches for, the 27 EU countries switched off (plausibly a
quarter to a third of the hobby 3D-printing audience, Germany above all),
and Shapr3D arriving on the Duo this month. Free-with-unlock utilities
typically convert 1 to 4% of downloads.

| Case | What happens | Downloads, year 1 | Sales | Proceeds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Floor | Submit and do nothing else | 100-300 | 1-6 | USD 8-50 |
| **Base** | A few honest posts (r/functionalprint, r/3Dprinting, r/BambuLab), Duo screenshots, captioned screenshots | 500-2,500 | **8-60** | **USD 70-500** |
| Lucky | A Duo editorial collection, or one clip that travels | 5,000-20,000 | 100-500 | USD 850-4,250 |

So the owner's USD 10-200 band is the base case, not pessimism: about 20
sales (USD 170) is a fair median *if he does the base-case work*. What
moves it, in order: (1) whether the product page carries the Duo badge
before Shapr3D's Duo release, (2) the first 10 ratings (a first-run that
works), (3) one post or video that shows a real printed part, (4) the EU.

## 4. App Review and listing risks

Ordered by how likely they are to bite. None needs a new build.

1. **"Made for iPhone Duo" in the description (APP_STORE.md:328).** That
   is the name of Apple's own badge ("Made for iPhone Duo", announced
   2026-10-22) and echoes Apple's "Made for iPhone" accessory mark. Saying it
   in text while the page has no badge (no Duo screenshots) invites a
   question under 2.3.1 (accuracy) or 5.2.5 (Apple trademarks). The claim is
   also unverified: the Duo's real screen edges have not been seen
   (LAUNCH_CHECKLIST.md:39-41; safe areas are estimates, TD-150,
   docs/TECHNICAL_DEBT.md:108). Fix: check build 54 on the owner's Duo, then
   write "On iPhone Duo: uses the whole inner display; folding keeps your
   work in place", or drop the line.
2. **Other unverified claims.** "The hand holding the Pencil does not move
   the view" (palm rejection: built, "to try on the iPad", docs/IPAD.md
   table). Small 2.3 risk; check it on the iPad before submitting.
3. **The screenshots.** Rendered on Windows (TD-95,
   docs/TECHNICAL_DEBT.md:88): Segoe UI instead of San Francisco, no status
   bar, no captions. App Review rarely objects (it is the real app's code),
   but they are weak marketing: iphone-1 shows a truncated hint ("✓ applies
   ...") and a clipped tool strip, ipad-1 shows "2 DOF". Only the first three
   appear in search results.
4. **The in-app purchase.** StoreKit 1 (`src/ui/ios/StoreKitStore.mm`,
   deprecated since iOS 18 but working). The classic rejection is "the
   reviewer could not load the product"; the active agreement and attaching
   the purchase to the version (checklist step 9) cover it. Restore is
   present. The unlock is a flag in the user defaults
   (`src/ui/Purchase.cpp:59-70`), no receipt check, so refunds keep the
   unlock: irrelevant at this scale.
5. **Privacy manifest.** The settings go through the user defaults, which
   `src/app/ios/PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` does not declare (planned for 0.3.1,
   PROJECT_STATUS.md, "0.3.1" item 1). No ITMS-91053 email came for build
   54; low risk.
6. **LGPL (Qt, OCCT, PlaneGCS statically linked).** The reasoning in
   docs/LICENSING.md is sound (complete source per tag, relinking by
   rebuilding, the VLC precedent). Two notes: LICENSING.md:432 says "we
   recommend the custom EULA anyway" and the owner chose Apple's standard
   EULA, which leaves the anti-circumvention waiver unsaid; and the residual
   risk (LICENSING.md:440-444) is a copyright holder asking Apple to remove
   the app. Low probability, remedy is an update. Not a review risk.
7. **Public source and the paywall.** MPL-2.0 lets anyone rebuild without
   the limit, and a fork under another name could appear on the store for
   free. More practical: the limit counts only `*.openshape` files at the
   top of the app folder (`src/ui/Purchase.cpp:42`), while a project in a
   subfolder of that folder opens in place without the gate
   (`src/ui/AppController.cpp:1001-1008`, `src/ui/IncomingFiles.cpp:151-154`).
   Moving projects into a folder in the Files app makes the limit
   disappear. Not worth a rebuild; count recursively (minus Exports, Inbox,
   .Trash) in 0.3.1. The reputational risk is bigger than the leak: someone
   on Reddit will call it "paywalled open source". Say plainly on the page
   and in the sheet that the unlock funds development of a free, open
   project; makers respond well to that.
8. **The name.** "FoldForm" is clear: no App Store app and no trademark
   found on 2026-11-01. But the bundle ID, repository, file extension and
   the source link in About all say "openshape", one word from OpenShape3D,
   whose kernel code FoldForm credits (THIRD_PARTY.md:19). Not a review
   issue; a little confusing for anyone who clicks through to GitHub.
9. **Version 0.3.0 on a paid product page.** Shoppers read "0.x" as beta.
   Call the next release 1.0.
10. **Wasted search space.** The keywords (APP_STORE.md:364) repeat
    "print,printing,cad", which the name already indexes (APP_STORE.md:186):
    17 of 100 bytes. Spend them on maker words, e.g.
    `caliper,measure,mount,spacer,knob,clip,insert,tolerance,hole`. The
    subtitle "Precise modeling for makers" (APP_STORE.md:187) carries no
    search term; "Parametric parts, STL and 3MF" does.
11. **The EU.** A business choice, not a review risk, but it removes a
    large part of the maker audience. After the first sales, reconsider
    trader status with a P.O. box.

## 5. Code and quality risks

- **Everything is tested on Windows; nothing runs on iOS.** `ci.yml` now
  compiles only (ci.yml:1-2; CLAUDE.md, "GitHub minutes are for compiling
  only"), so even the macOS tests and the Mac real-UI run that used to run
  in CI no longer do. The iOS-only code (StoreKit, 325 lines; the share
  sheet; opening files from other apps with security-scoped access, TD-69,
  docs/TECHNICAL_DEBT.md:73; the POSIX fault dispatcher that turns kernel
  crashes into failed steps, `KernelUtil.h:76-81`) is exercised only by the
  owner's hand tries. The 2026-10-30 review said the same
  (PROJECT_STATUS.md:177).
- **The Duo work has never been checked on a Duo.** The `duo` scenario
  (`src/app/acceptance/Duo.cpp`) tests Windows windows at guessed sizes and
  insets (TD-150). Fold-aware placement does not exist (TD-140,
  docs/TECHNICAL_DEBT.md:104): half folded, the value box or keypad may sit
  on the crease. The tap-to-select fix is untried on a device.
- **Speed and memory on a phone are unmeasured** (docs/IPAD.md:302). The
  commit after an edit still recomputes on the GUI thread, about 190 ms on
  the 249-face enclosure on a desktop i5 (TD-1, docs/TECHNICAL_DEBT.md:7;
  PROJECT_STATUS.md:910). STEP import accepts up to 512 MB and 2,000 solids
  (`Exchange.h:41-44`), sized for a desktop; on an iPhone a big assembly is
  more likely to be ended by the system than refused politely.
- **Topological naming** picks the nearest signature with no ambiguity
  check (TD-3, docs/TECHNICAL_DEBT.md:9). The description promises "every
  step stays editable"; the first time a user edits step 2 and the fillet
  from step 5 lands on the wrong edge of a symmetric part, that promise
  costs a star.
- **Concentration.** `InteractionController.cpp` 5,369 lines,
  `SketchSession.cpp` 3,646, `Operation.cpp` 2,593, `AppController.cpp`
  2,517, `Main.qml` 1,670. Fine while one person and his agents hold it in
  their heads; expensive for every UI change.
- **The documentation outweighs the product surface.** ARCHITECTURE.md is
  139 KB, TECHNICAL_DEBT.md 79 KB, PROJECT_STATUS.md 70 KB, APP_STORE.md
  57 KB, while the app has no tool icons, no onboarding and no samples. The
  engineering is ahead of the storefront; the next hours should go to what a
  user sees in the first two minutes.

## 6. The Duo bet

**The facts as of 2026-11-01.** The iPhone Duo went on sale 2026-10-23 at
USD 1,999; Counterpoint forecast about 6 million units in 2026 (a forecast,
not sales). Apple added a "Made for iPhone Duo" badge and launched Duo
editorial collections on sale day, with 50-plus optimized apps (FoldForm
was not on the store yet). App Store Connect accepts Duo screenshots now
(outer 1398 x 2034, inner 2007 x 2853; APP_STORE.md:419-425), they earn the
badge, and they become mandatory in April 2027. **Shapr3D announced on
2026-10-27 that full modeling with Apple Pencil comes to the Duo in
November.** The window in which FoldForm is the obvious precise-CAD app for
the Duo is a few weeks at most, and it closes against an app with 34,000
ratings.

That does not make the bet wrong. Shapr3D will be a USD 299-a-year
professional tool on the Duo; FoldForm can be the USD 9.99, no-account,
made-for-printing one. But "also runs on the Duo" is not a position. These
are things a Duo-tailored CAD app can do that nobody does today:

1. **Half-folded workbench.** Stand the Duo in its laptop pose: the top
   half shows the part, the bottom half becomes a full keypad, the value box
   and the step history, like a CAD calculator next to the printer. The iOS
   27.1 SDK has the pieces (`UIHingeInteraction`, `UIViewReservedRegion`,
   named in TD-140). This is the shot for a video.
2. **Closed to measure, open to model.** On the outer display, a "measure"
   view lists a part's named sizes (a spacer's outer and inner diameter and
   height; a bracket's hole spacing) for typing caliper readings standing at
   the broken thing; unfold and the full model is there with those values.
   Jigsmith markets the calipers story; FoldForm can own it by posture.
3. **Sketch and solid side by side along the crease.** The inner display is
   almost square: the 2D sketch on one half (finger or Pencil), the live 3D
   result on the other, both views of one document. Shapr3D has not shown
   this.
4. **Split View with the printer app.** Drag the 3MF out of FoldForm into
   the printer or file app on the other half (a native drag interaction
   beside the share sheet).

**Is FoldForm positioned to be featured for the device?** Not today: no Duo
screenshots (so no badge), Windows-rendered screenshots without captions,
no app preview, a text-only UI, zero ratings, and it missed the launch-day
collections. What would put it in the running: the badge, a 20-30 second
preview recorded on the Duo (docs/MARKETING_RENDERS.md, piece 1, adapted),
a featuring nomination in App Store Connect if the account offers it, and
the story Apple's editors like: one developer, open source, no account, no
subscription, made for the new device.

## 7. The one change, and five ideas ranked

### The one change: make the product page a Duo product page now, before Shapr3D's Duo version

The owner has a Duo, and build 54 is built with the iOS 27.1 SDK. Before
pressing Submit: install build 54 on the Duo, fold and unfold with a part
open (docs/IPAD.md, "The iPhone Duo", steps 1-5); if nothing sits under the
side bar, take the inner and outer screenshots on the device and add them to
this submission, fix the description line (section 4, item 1), and send the
log. If something is under the side bar, submit without the Duo line and
ship 0.3.1 with the log's fixes within days, with the Duo screenshots. It
costs an hour, needs no code, and it is the only lever that changes who sees
the app in the weeks that matter.

### Five ideas, ranked

| # | Idea | Size | Why this rank |
|---|---|---|---|

Captions alone tend to lift conversion from the search results, where only three screenshots show |

autosave for named projects (TD-51, already planned), the user-defaults
entry in the privacy manifest, counting projects in subfolders, and calling
the release 1.0.

## 8. Verdict

**Ship after two metadata-only changes:** fix or drop the "Made for iPhone
Duo" line, and add Duo screenshots taken on the owner's Duo if build 54
looks right there. Do not hold for code: nothing in the review risks needs a
new build. Then spend November on the Duo page and the first minute of use,
not on the engine; expect tens of sales, not hundreds, unless Apple
features it.
